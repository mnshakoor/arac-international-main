Executive Assessment | BLUF

Three developments materially changed the strategic picture.

First, northern Ethiopia has moved from post-conflict deterioration into renewed major warfare. Tigrayan forces seized three airports and clashed with federal forces across multiple locations. Telecommunications were subsequently disrupted across Tigray. The conflict is unfolding alongside deteriorating Ethiopia-Eritrea relations, Sudan's war, disputes over Red Sea access, and an emerging coalition linking armed organizations that previously fought on opposing sides. [1][2]

Second, the DRC Ebola assessment requires a significant downward correction in confidence regarding containment. As of September 25, Congo had recorded 7,820 confirmed cases and 3,779 deaths. Health authorities had identified only about 30,000 potential contacts against an estimated requirement exceeding 400,000. More than 80 percent of new confirmed infections were occurring among people not already on contact lists. North Kivu has simultaneously emerged as a major new hotspot, with treatment facilities in Butembo reaching capacity. [3][4]

Third, the Sahel security picture has worsened materially. JNIM killed more than 100 Malian soldiers during its September 10 assault on Dioura, reportedly took more than 100 troops hostage, subjected Sévaré airport to six consecutive nights of shelling, and appeared to encircle the military position at Konna. [5] The combination of mass casualties, prisoners, persistent indirect fire against aviation infrastructure, and pressure on multiple military positions demonstrates increasing ability to impose operational dilemmas on the Malian state.

The Middle East remains the highest global systemic vulnerability, but its trajectory has become more uncertain. Iran has proposed a seven-day sequence that could reopen the Strait of Hormuz and pause regional fighting. U.S.-Iranian discussions through intermediaries were described as positive and constructive, but reporting on September 26 indicated that President Donald Trump remained skeptical of the proposal. Iran simultaneously says it will not make concessions on its nuclear program. [6][7]

In Sudan, the most important weekly signal is economic rather than territorial. The Sudanese pound has fallen from approximately 4,100 per U.S. dollar in May to around 7,500 on the black market in army-held territory. Reuters describes an increasingly divided economic geography in which important gold, livestock, oilseed, and gum arabic production remains outside effective SAF-controlled fiscal channels. Roughly five million displaced Sudanese have returned to their homes, increasing demand precisely as purchasing power and state revenue deteriorate. [8]

In Ukraine, systems warfare continues in both directions. Ukrainian strikes have repeatedly disrupted Russian refinery capacity, including the Moscow and Novoshakhtinsk facilities. Russian strikes, meanwhile, forced Ukraine's largest steel plant to halt production after four attacks in five weeks caused extensive damage and killed workers. [9][10] The relevant strategic metric is increasingly strike rate versus repair and substitution rate.

The Taiwan Strait entered a new diplomatic phase following the Washington summit between Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping. No major public breakthrough emerged on Taiwan. Taipei immediately emphasized that continuing U.S. arms transfers serve U.S. interests by strengthening Taiwan's defense and protecting the semiconductor ecosystem. A pending U.S. weapons package therefore becomes an important post-summit indicator. [11]

Finally, Somalia should remain elevated as an emerging humanitarian-security flashpoint. IPC analysis indicates approximately 26,000 displaced people around Baidoa could face famine conditions during October-December under a severe flooding and insufficient-aid scenario. More than five million Somalis already face crisis-level food insecurity or worse, while projected food assistance would reach only around 1.3 percent of those in need. [12]

Key Judgments

Analytic Judgments

KJ-1 | High Confidence: Ethiopia has crossed a meaningful escalation threshold. The principal risk is now conflict linkage among Tigrayan forces, federal forces, Fano formations, Eritrea, other Ethiopian armed organizations, Sudan-border dynamics, and regional geopolitical competition.
KJ-2 | High Confidence: DRC's Ebola outbreak cannot presently be characterized as approaching national control. Improvements in parts of Ituri coexist with geographic redistribution, severe contact-tracing deficits, saturated treatment capacity, mistrust, and new transmission zones.
KJ-3 | High Confidence: JNIM's September operations demonstrate an ability to impose significant losses on Mali's conventional forces while simultaneously pressuring military aviation and logistics infrastructure.
KJ-4 | Moderate Confidence: A diplomatic pathway exists for reducing Middle Eastern maritime risk, but the probability of near-term implementation has declined because Washington and Tehran remain divided over the scope and credibility of any agreement.
KJ-5 | High Confidence: Sudan increasingly demonstrates the difference between territorial recovery and functional sovereignty. Currency stability, revenue capture, markets, services, infrastructure, and civilian purchasing power should receive analytical weight comparable to front-line geography.
KJ-6 | High Confidence: Russian and Ukrainian strategic-strike campaigns are increasingly producing measurable economic-system effects. Recuperability, not simply physical vulnerability, is becoming the decisive variable.
KJ-7 | Moderate-High Confidence: Taiwan remains embedded in U.S.-China bargaining, but the Washington summit did not publicly alter the core deterrence architecture. The pending U.S. arms package is therefore a high-value indicator.

01 | Ethiopia-Tigray-Eritrea

Conflict Linkage Becomes the Primary Risk

Fact

Tigrayan forces seized three airports on September 23 while fighting federal forces across multiple locations. The offensive effectively shattered the containment architecture created by the 2022 Pretoria Agreement. [1]

Telecommunications were disrupted across Tigray on September 25. [2]

The TPLF has also aligned with six other armed organizations. Reuters reports that the Ethiopian government accuses Eritrea of financially supporting members of this alliance, an allegation Eritrea denies. [1]

Interpretation
The problem is no longer adequately represented as Addis Ababa versus Tigray.

The emerging system is: Tigray conflict + Amhara armed actors + Eritrean rivalry + multiple Ethiopian insurgencies + Sudan instability + Red Sea competition.

Each additional linkage increases pathways for escalation.
QAP Analytic Judgment

High Confidence: Ethiopia has returned to active major-conflict status.

Moderate-High Confidence: Telecommunications disruption will significantly degrade independent verification, humanitarian coordination, civilian warning, commerce, and situational awareness.

Moderate Confidence: The anti-government coalition can translate political alignment into sustained operational coordination.

Moderate-High Confidence: Direct Eritrean military intervention remains the most consequential escalation threshold.

Indicators & Warnings

Eritrean deployments southward; sustained airport control; fighting approaching Mekelle; Tigray-Fano operational coordination; federal reserve mobilization; prolonged communications disruption; humanitarian-access restrictions; displacement toward Sudan; attacks on major transportation corridors.

02 | DRC

The Outbreak Is Moving Faster Than Surveillance

Fact

Africa CDC Director-General Jean Kaseya said health authorities had identified approximately 30,000 contacts when more than 400,000 would normally be expected given roughly 7,000 confirmed cases. More than 80 percent of newly confirmed infections were found among people who were not already being monitored. [3]

WHO reported that Ebola had reached 63 health zones across seven provinces by September 25. [3] Government data recorded 7,820 confirmed cases and 3,779 deaths as of September 22. [4]

North Kivu now accounts for approximately one-third of new confirmed cases and deaths. All 29 beds at one Butembo treatment center were occupied this week, prompting authorities to open additional facilities. [4]

Interpretation
Localized improvement in Ituri does not equal national containment. The outbreak is undergoing geographic redistribution.
QAP Analytic Judgment

High Confidence: Surveillance is currently insufficient to characterize the majority of transmission chains.

High Confidence: DRC is facing a compound epidemiological, governance, trust, access, and capacity crisis.

Moderate-High Confidence: North Kivu's dense population, mobility, armed conflict, and mistrust could create a new transmission center even if conditions improve elsewhere.

RCA/CPS Insight

The Butembo experience demonstrates why public trust is operational infrastructure. A health facility was burned after residents accused it of collaborating with Ebola responders. Some residents are refusing screening measures. [4]

trusted information + community intermediaries + surveillance + access + treatment + safe burial + mobility monitoring
03 | Sahel

JNIM Demonstrates Operational Depth

Fact

JNIM attacked the military camp at Dioura on September 10. Two Reuters sources estimated that between 120 and 130 Malian soldiers were killed. More than 100 were reportedly taken hostage. [5]

JNIM subsequently claimed attacks against air-defense infrastructure at Sévaré airport. Residents reported six consecutive nights of shelling. JNIM fighters also appeared to have encircled the military camp at Konna. [5]

Interpretation
The important development is the combination of capabilities: mass-casualty assault + prisoner capture + sustained indirect fire + aviation suppression + maneuver around fixed military positions.
QAP Analytic Judgment

High Confidence: JNIM can impose serious tactical defeats on Malian conventional forces.

Moderate-High Confidence: Pressure against Sévaré indicates increasing emphasis on degrading aviation and drone advantages available to government forces.

Moderate Confidence: JNIM currently possesses the capability to seize and sustainably administer major central Malian urban centers. That remains unproven.

Warning Indicators

Loss of Konna; evacuation of additional military camps; persistent shelling of Sévaré; destruction of aircraft; disruption of Mopti-Sévaré logistics; increased hostage bargaining; JNIM-FLA coordination; declining Africa Corps presence.

04 | Middle East

Diplomacy Exists, but the Window Is Narrow

Fact

Iran submitted a proposal through Qatari mediators that could reopen Hormuz and pause regional hostilities within seven days. U.S. officials had characterized indirect discussions as positive and constructive. [6]

However, reporting on September 26 indicated Trump remained skeptical of the proposal. Iran separately stated that it would not compromise on its nuclear program. [7]

Interpretation

The diplomatic architecture currently contains two different negotiations: maritime access and cessation of hostilities, versus the underlying nuclear and regional-security dispute. A narrow agreement on Hormuz may therefore be achievable without resolving the strategic rivalry.

QAP Analytic Judgment

Moderate Confidence: A limited maritime arrangement remains achievable.

Low-Moderate Confidence: Such an arrangement would rapidly produce a broader U.S.-Iran political settlement.

High Confidence: Houthi operations and Saudi security remain potential independent spoilers.

05 | Sudan

The Economy Reveals the Limits of Territorial Victory

Fact

The Sudanese pound trades near 7,500 per dollar on the black market in army-controlled territory, compared with approximately 4,100 in May and around 600 before the war. [8]

Important gold, livestock, oilseed, and gum arabic production remains in areas controlled by the RSF. Sudan's official gold sector reported approximately 70 tons of production last year, but only 14.7 tons appeared as central-bank exports. [8]

Approximately five million of the 14 million people displaced during the war have returned home, primarily to areas retaken by the SAF. Their return is adding demand to already constrained markets.

Interpretation
Military geography and economic geography have diverged. The SAF can regain territory while failing to regain revenue + foreign currency + productive assets + purchasing power + market confidence + public services.
QAP Analytic Judgment

High Confidence: Economic partition is becoming a principal obstacle to stabilization.

High Confidence: Currency performance should be incorporated into Sudan early-warning monitoring.

Moderate-High Confidence: Large-scale returns without economic recovery could generate secondary displacement or urban instability.

06 | Ukraine

Strike Rate Versus Recovery Rate

Fact

A September 20 Ukrainian attack halted crude processing at Moscow's major refinery and damaged important distillation units. Repairs were expected to require weeks. [9]

A September 25 drone attack also suspended operations at Russia's Novoshakhtinsk refinery. [10]

Russia is simultaneously attacking Ukrainian productive capacity. Ukraine's largest steel plant halted production after four Russian strikes in five weeks caused extensive damage and killed five workers. Russian strikes are increasingly affecting industrial facilities, logistics networks, Black Sea infrastructure and civilian systems. [13]

Interpretation

Both states are increasingly targeting the other's ability to regenerate war-supporting economic capacity.

strike tempo > repair + substitution = cumulative degradation
QAP Analytic Judgment

High Confidence: Ukraine's refinery campaign is imposing measurable economic costs.

High Confidence: Russian strikes are imposing comparable systemic pressure on Ukraine's smaller industrial base.

Moderate-High Confidence: Ukraine is more vulnerable to individual industrial losses because its substitution capacity is structurally smaller.

07 | Mozambique

No New Strategic Inflection

No sufficiently corroborated development during the reporting window changes the standing Mozambique assessment. ISM's September movement from Cabo Delgado through Montepuez and Balama into Niassa remains the principal current signal.

QAP Analytic Judgment

High Confidence: Administrative boundaries remain a poor proxy for insurgent operating geography.

Moderate-High Confidence: Niassa remains a penetration zone rather than established ISM territory.

Moderate Confidence: Another substantial Niassa operation during the next 60 to 90 days would indicate development of a recurring western operating corridor.

Collection Priority

Cabo Delgado → Montepuez → Balama → Niassa → informal mining areas → conservation zones → road networks
08 | Taiwan Strait

Summit Diplomacy Leaves the Core Question Unresolved

Fact

The Trump-Xi Washington summit concluded without a major public breakthrough regarding Taiwan. [11]

Xi urged Washington to oppose Taiwanese independence. Taiwan's deputy foreign minister responded that continuing U.S. arms transfers serves American interests by strengthening Taiwan's defense and preserving its semiconductor contribution to the global economy. A pending U.S. arms package remains unresolved.

Interpretation

The Taiwan issue is increasingly embedded inside a wider bargaining system involving trade, semiconductors, military sales, technology, diplomatic signaling, and deterrence.

QAP Analytic Judgment

Moderate-High Confidence: Beijing will continue attempting to convert broader U.S.-China negotiations into leverage over Taiwan arms transfers.

High Confidence: Washington's treatment of the pending weapons package is now a particularly valuable post-summit indicator.

Emerging Flashpoint | Somalia

Humanitarian Contraction Meets Security Fragility

IPC estimates that roughly 26,000 displaced people around Baidoa could face famine during October-December under a severe flooding and insufficient-aid scenario. [12]

More than five million Somalis already face crisis-level food insecurity or worse. Projected food assistance during October-December would reach only approximately 70,400 people per month, around 1.3 percent of those in need. Approximately 1.92 million children are expected to require treatment for acute malnutrition through next June.

QAP Analytic Judgment

High Confidence: Humanitarian financing has become a security variable.

Moderate-High Confidence: Severe food insecurity around displaced populations can increase migration, armed-group recruitment, local competition and pressure on already fragile institutions.

CARVER Judgment

The most important change is not the ranking. It is the reason several systems score highly.

  • DRC: the response cannot rapidly substitute hundreds of thousands of missing surveillance relationships.
  • Ethiopia: political, communications, aviation and security systems are failing simultaneously.
  • Sudan: territorial control cannot rapidly recreate revenue and market confidence.
  • Mali: conventional military positions increasingly depend upon aviation and logistics nodes that JNIM is actively pressuring.

Root Cause Analysis / Conflict Prevention & Stabilization

RCA/CPS

This week's evidence produces a useful cross-theater refinement: Resilience depends upon legitimate systems detecting deterioration before disruption exceeds their ability to respond.

Ethiopia

Root causes: incomplete Pretoria implementation, unresolved territorial claims, wartime grievances, Tigrayan political fragmentation, Amhara-Tigray competition, Ethiopia-Eritrea rivalry.

Prevention: stop conflict linkage.

Stabilization: restore communications, aviation, humanitarian access and functioning political monitoring.

Positive Peace: Well-Functioning Government; Good Relations with Neighbors; Free Flow of Information; Acceptance of the Rights of Others.

DRC

Root causes: weak territorial governance, armed conflict, displacement, institutional mistrust and insufficient healthcare capacity.

Prevention: rebuild surveillance faster than transmission spreads.

Stabilization: trusted community engagement must accompany clinical capacity.

MMEL: percentage of new cases already on contact lists; affected health zones; treatment-bed occupancy; time to isolation; healthcare-worker infections.

Sahel

Root causes: governance deficits, civilian harm, exclusion, militant financing, rural state weakness and cross-border sanctuary.

Prevention: protect aviation and logistics nodes while disrupting JNIM financing and mobility.

Stabilization: civilian protection and legitimate local administration must accompany military operations.

Sudan

Root causes: fragmented coercive institutions, militarized economic networks, external patronage and unequal control over productive resources.

Prevention: constrain external military enablement.

Stabilization: rebuild fiscal sovereignty alongside territorial sovereignty.

Middle East

Prevention: pursue a narrow, verifiable maritime agreement even if the nuclear dispute remains unresolved.

MMEL: Hormuz traffic, tanker insurance, Saudi infrastructure attacks, Houthi operations and Iranian compliance.

Mozambique

Prevention: deny insurgent mobility rather than protecting isolated high-value sites.

Stabilization: integrate security, rural governance, justice and legitimate local economic opportunity.

Ukraine

The strategic resilience question is: Can legitimate economic function recover faster than the adversary can disrupt it?

Distributed production, repair capacity, air defense, spare parts and infrastructure redundancy therefore become stabilization assets.

Taiwan

Taiwan's strategy represents prevention through distributed resilience. The objective is not invulnerability. It is ensuring that disruption cannot rapidly produce political paralysis.

Analyst's Notebook

Assumptions, Alternatives & Intelligence Gaps

Key assumptions

Ethiopia's fighting persists beyond a short tactical episode; Eritrea has not yet entered the war directly at scale; DRC surveillance figures accurately reflect a major contact-tracing deficit; JNIM's Dioura losses inflicted on Mali are broadly within the range reported by independent sources; U.S.-Iran indirect diplomacy remains active; Sudan's currency deterioration reflects structural fragmentation; Russia and Ukraine retain long-range strike capability; ISM has not established durable control in Niassa; Washington has not fundamentally altered Taiwan policy following the summit.

Alternative hypotheses

Ethiopia's offensive could culminate quickly and reopen negotiations. DRC's contact deficit may partly reflect incomplete administrative consolidation of existing tracing records. JNIM's tactical successes may exceed its capacity for territorial governance. Washington and Tehran could still reach a narrow Hormuz agreement rapidly. Sudan's currency could stabilize through external financing. Russia may restore refinery capacity faster than current estimates. Niassa may remain an episodic ISM theater.

Priority intelligence gaps

Eritrean force posture; actual control of Tigrayan airports; Ethiopian coalition command relationships; DRC locality-level transmission chains; JNIM prisoner disposition and Konna posture; U.S. response to Iran's Hormuz proposal; Sudan foreign-exchange reserves and gold flows; Russian repair capacity; ISM logistics inside Niassa; U.S. decision on Taiwan arms.

Strategic Outlook

30, 90 and 180-Day Outlook

30-Day Outlook

Ethiopia, DRC and Mali are the principal African escalation watches. Ethiopia is likely to experience continued military confrontation while actors test the balance created by the Tigrayan offensive. DRC's outbreak will continue shifting geographically unless surveillance improves rapidly. Mali is likely to reinforce Sévaré and Konna while JNIM attempts to exploit momentum. The Middle East may produce either the week's largest positive strategic change through a Hormuz arrangement or renewed escalation if negotiations fail.

Confidence: High.

90-Day Outlook

The decisive Ethiopian question will be whether conflict remains primarily federal-Tigrayan or regionalizes. In DRC, the key measure will be whether the proportion of new infections already captured by contact tracing rises substantially. In Mali, the key question is whether JNIM converts tactical victories into persistent pressure on major transport, aviation and administrative nodes. Repeated ISM operations in Niassa would justify formally expanding Mozambique's core conflict geography. Ukraine's winter period will provide an increasingly measurable test of industrial and energy recuperability.

Most likely global condition: Prolonged Competitive Instability.
Confidence: Moderate-High.

Bottom Line

Where is our ability to observe and respond deteriorating faster than the threat itself?

This week's strongest warning comes from the DRC. The number that matters most is not 7,820 confirmed Ebola cases. It is the gap between approximately 30,000 identified contacts and more than 400,000 that epidemiological expectations suggest should be monitored. [3] That gap represents unseen transmission.

Ethiopia presents the same problem in another form. Communications disruption creates unseen military movement, unseen civilian harm and degraded humanitarian awareness.

Mali's security system faces a related challenge. JNIM is increasingly attacking not only troops but the aviation and logistics architecture that allows the state to see, move and respond.

Sudan's currency collapse reveals economic deterioration that territorial maps cannot show.

Across these apparently different crises, the underlying systems principle is the same: Strategic failure frequently begins when institutions lose the ability to detect, understand and respond to change faster than the threat environment evolves.

For QAP, this means collection capacity is not merely an intelligence function. It is itself a resilience variable. For CARVER, it means Recuperability must account for the information, logistics and institutional systems required to restore function. For RCA/CPS, it means prevention cannot begin only after violence crosses a threshold.

The most valuable early-warning question may be simpler: Where is our ability to observe and respond deteriorating faster than the threat itself? That is where the next strategic surprise is most likely to emerge.
