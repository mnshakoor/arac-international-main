Overview

Teaching Positive Peace as a Working Framework

The Institute for Economics and Peace developed the Positive Peace framework from a straightforward empirical question: what do the most peaceful societies have in common that the least peaceful ones lack? The answer was not primarily the absence of conflict. It was the presence of eight measurable conditions, among them a well functioning government, sound business environment, free flow of information, good relations with neighbours, and equitable distribution of resources.

That finding has a practical consequence. If peace has identifiable structural components, then organizations that are not conflict specialists still contribute to it or erode it through ordinary decisions about hiring, procurement, information sharing, and community relations. ARAC International teaches this framework so that partners can see where their existing work already sits inside it, and where a modest change in practice would strengthen it.

Our founder is a certified IEP Ambassador and a certified Human Rights Consultant through the United States Institute of Diplomacy and Human Rights. That training informs how the material is delivered, but the content is adapted to the audience rather than presented as a fixed curriculum. A district health authority, a mining sector employer, and a community youth organization need the same framework explained through different examples.

What We Deliver

Learning Formats

01

Positive Peace Workshops

Half day and full day sessions introducing the eight pillars, the evidence behind them, and a structured exercise in which participants locate their own organization within the framework.

02

Human Rights in Practice

Applied training on integrating rights based principles into policy, procurement, employment practice, and community engagement, aimed at staff who make operational decisions rather than legal specialists.

03

SDG Literacy for Teams

Sessions that make the Sustainable Development Goals usable at team level, with emphasis on Goal 16 and on the reporting obligations partners are likely to face.

04

Mediation Foundations

Introductory and intermediate training in dispute resolution technique for staff, community representatives, and volunteer mediators, including practice sessions and structured feedback.

05

Information Integrity and Media Literacy

Practical instruction in source evaluation, verification habits, and recognising manipulation in circulating information. Delivered in partnership with our public learning platform.

06

Board and Leadership Briefings

Short, senior level sessions for boards and executive teams who need enough of the framework to govern well without a full training commitment.

Design Principles

How Our Training Is Built

  • Adapted, not recycled. Material is rewritten for the sector, region, and seniority of the audience before delivery.
  • Evidence forward. Claims made in a session are attributed to their source so that participants can check them afterward.
  • Applied over theoretical. Every session includes work on a real problem the participants brought with them.
  • Accessible language. We avoid specialist vocabulary that excludes the community members these programmes exist to serve.
  • Follow up included. A review session at an agreed interval after delivery, because a single workshop rarely changes practice on its own.

Public Learning Resources

Beyond commissioned training, ARAC maintains open learning materials on media literacy, digital literacy, and critical thinking for general audiences. These are free to use and require no organizational relationship with us.

Our published research and editorial work sit alongside the training programme and are available through the resource platform.
